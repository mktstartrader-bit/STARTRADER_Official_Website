FAQ

Commodities trading questions

What is commodity trading and how does it work?
Commodity trading is speculating on the price of raw materials — metals such as gold and copper, energies such as crude oil and natural gas, and agricultural products such as wheat and coffee. With CFDs you never take delivery of the physical goods: you open a position on the price direction, go long or short, and settle the difference in cash when you close.
Which commodities can I trade with STARTRADER?
You can trade 25+ commodity CFDs across three sectors — metals (gold, silver, platinum, palladium and copper), energies (WTI, Brent and natural gas) and agriculture (wheat, corn, coffee, sugar, cocoa and soybeans) — all on MT4, MT5 and the STAR platforms.
What are the trading hours for commodities?
Metals and energies trade almost 24 hours a day, five days a week, with a short daily break. Agricultural markets follow their exchange sessions and are open for a shorter window each day. Exact hours per instrument are shown in the platform's contract specifications.
What leverage is available on commodities?
Flexible leverage is available up to 1:1000, letting you take a larger position with less capital. Higher leverage magnifies losses as well as gains, so use stop-losses and size positions carefully. Leverage above 1:30 may not be available in certain regions due to regulatory restrictions.
Which platform features help with commodity trading?
Real-time pricing, interactive charts and technical analysis tools, a live news feed, market, limit and stop orders, stop-loss and take-profit levels, portfolio tracking, custom price alerts and a demo mode for practising strategies risk-free — all available on desktop, web and mobile.
Are commodities a good hedge against inflation?
Hard assets such as gold and oil have historically held value during inflationary periods, which is why many traders use them to balance a portfolio. Past performance is never a guarantee of future results, and commodity prices can be volatile — so treat any hedge as one part of a wider risk-management plan.
