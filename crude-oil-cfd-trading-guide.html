Crude Oil CFDs: How To Trade Oil Online In India

Learn how crude oil CFD trading works, from WTI vs Brent benchmarks to price drivers, margin, and the steps to open your first oil position.

STARTRADER Research DeskPublished 22 August 2026 · 13 min read
Link copied

Crude oil CFDs offer traders the opportunity to speculate on global oil prices without owning physical barrels of oil or dealing with futures contracts. Have you ever wondered how retail traders take positions on global energy shifts without ever handling a barrel of oil? That’s the opportunity CFD crude oil trading offers.

Oil remains the world’s single most important commodity. CFDs (Contracts for Difference) is an agreement between you and the provider whose value tracks a benchmark oil prices. On most platforms, traders can take both long and short positions to adapt to fluctuating market conditions.

For the beginners, the two main things to grasp are the benchmarks, WTI and Brent, and what moves them, from OPEC+ policy to geopolitical shifts, are the things they must mainly grasp. This guide details the instruments, price drivers, procedures and risks. Similarly, traders can also trade gold in a similar way, through CFDs. .

Quick answer

A crude oil CFD is a derivative contract that allows traders to speculate on the price movements of oil without having to physically purchase barrels or go into the futures market themselves. To trade oil online, a trader usually opens a supported account, finds a WTI or Brent instrument, analyzes the price chart, decides on a trade size, applies risk controls, and monitors the position until it is closed. Oil CFDs are leveraged products and carry a high risk of loss.

What Is A CFD On Crude Oil?

A CFD on crude oil is a contract to exchange the difference between the price of an oil benchmark when the contract opens and when it closes.

With this instrument, you only take a speculation on the change in price. You don’t own barrels, schedule commodity delivery or store any physical product. Your outcome is determined by the difference between the price you enter at and the price you exit at, subject to applicable costs and charges. .

What You Own

You own a contract position instead of tangible energy resources. There’s no physical delivery, shipping requirement or refinery handling at any stage.

Main Oil Benchmarks

Most online platforms provide products based on two different global benchmarks:

  • WTI Crude Oil: The leading US crude benchmark.
  • Brent Crude Oil: A global benchmark widely used for international energy pricing.

Long And Short Exposure

CFDs may be allowed depending on platform functionality:

  • Long Position: Buying in order to potentially benefit from an increase in the underlying price.
  • Short Position: Selling in order to potentially benefit from a decrease in the underlying price.

This is a mechanical feature rather than a recommendation to buy or sell.

Leverage And Margin

These instruments are generally traded on margin, which means you only need a fraction of the total trade value (margin) to open a position. Leverage increases the potential for profit and loss and is a major risk factor.

Learn what is a CFD for more on this type of trading structure.

CFD On WTI Crude Oil Vs Brent: Which Benchmark Are You Trading?

WTI and Brent are two regional oil markets that are distinct from one another in terms of price movements and supply and demand factors.

What Is WTI Crude Oil?

WTI is the U.S. crude oil benchmark. The price is strongly influenced by domestic supply, inventories at Cushing, Oklahoma, US shale production trends and domestic refinery demand.

What Is Brent Crude Oil

Brent is the world’s chief crude benchmark, setting prices worldwide. It is very sensitive to changes in global supply and demand, disruptions to shipping routes, and international geopolitical sentiment.

Crude Oil CFD Price: What Moves Oil Prices?

Global supply, macroeconomic demand, geopolitical tension, US dollar valuation and seasonal consumption patterns drive the CFD crude oil price.

OPEC+ Production Decisions

OPEC+ production decisions influence a large part of the global supply. News of production cuts or increased output has a huge impact on market sentiment. OPEC member countries collectively hold close to 80% of the world’s proven crude oil reserves, giving the group significant long-term pricing leverage.

US Inventory Data

US Energy Information Administration (EIA) weekly inventory data show crude levels. Stock builds are indicative of weak demand or oversupply while inventory draws are indicative of a tighter market.

Geopolitical Risk

Aggressive reactions in energy markets can be influenced by international conflict, economic sanctions, threats to pipelines or instability in key producing and transit regions.

Global Demand

Consumption is a function of economic health. Data shows that oil demand is influenced by the health of global manufacturing, aviation, shipping and the growth of massive economies like China, India and the OECD.

US Dollar Strength

Oil prices are priced in U.S. dollars worldwide, so a stronger dollar means oil costs more for foreign buyers, often leading to downward pressure on prices. A weaker dollar can help support prices, but the inverse relationship is not a hard and fast rule.

Seasonal Demand

Consumption is seasonal. Petrol demand usually increases during the summer driving season, while heating oil demand tends to rise during cold winters.

Note: Market fundamentals are interconnected. Price is not driven by one thing but a constant mix of inventory data, macroeconomic demand, geopolitical stress and currency movements.

How To Trade Crude Oil Online: Step By Step

To trade oil online, you need to have a supported account, locate your benchmark of choice, examine the chart and place an order of the correct size.

Step 1: Open A Supported Trading Account

You’ll need a multi-asset or regular trading account with a provider such as STARTRADER, that provides energy instruments.

Step 2: Complete Verification

Live market access needs an identity verification. Most brokers offer demo accounts to practice with no risk.

Step 3: Fund The Account If Trading Live

You need capital to put into live environments. Positions must be supported by sufficient margin. If available margin falls below the required level, positions may be closed automatically.

Step 4: Search For WTI Oil Or Brent Oil

Locate the instrument. It might be called WTI, US Oil, Brent, UK Oil or Crude Oil, depending on the conventions of the platform.

Step 5: Open The Oil Chart

The chart gives you historical price action, and lets you choose appropriate timeframes and apply analytical tools.

Step 6: Choose Buy Or Sell

  • Buy: Check this box if you expect the price to go up.
  • Sell: Check this box if you expect the price to go down.

Step 7: Choose Trade Size

The size of the lot you choose to trade will determine your exposure to the market, how much margin you’ll need to hold and the monetary effect of each pip movement.

Step 8: Add Stop Loss And Take Profit If Needed

These automated risk management tools allow you to set your exit parameters and may help manage the impact of adverse price movements.

Step 9: Review The Order

Verify the symbol, trade size, margin requirements, spreads, overnight financing charges and your risk limits before you confirm.

Step 10: Monitor And Close The Position

Monitor the floating equity closely, upcoming economic news, margin levels and overall account health until the trade is closed.

How To Read A Crude Oil Price Chart

A crude oil chart is a visual display of the price history of a benchmark in dollars per barrel, which can help you identify trends, volatility and important market levels.

What The Chart Shows

The graph shows the price of a commodity (WTI, Brent, etc.) in US dollars per barrel over time, and how it changes.

Basics of Candlestick

  • Each candle is a duration of a specific timeframe for price action.
  • The body displays the open and close prices.
  • The wicks show the high and low prices reached.
  • Longer candles suggest high momentum and volatility.

Support And Resistance

In the past, support was a price floor where buying interest came in. Resistance is a historical ceiling where selling pressure has built up in the past.

News Timing

Sharp price moves often follow inventory reports, OPEC+ announcements, macroeconomic data and sudden geopolitical headlines.

Avoid

Don’t believe in “perfect” time frames, guaranteed technical patterns or random entry/exit points.

Key Risks Of Trading Oil CFDs

Derivatives trading exposes individuals to leverage amplification, sudden gap risk, overnight financing costs and extreme news-driven volatility.

Leverage Risk

Leverage raises exposure. Small changes in the underlying price can lead to large losses, because profits and losses are calculated on the full position value rather than the margin deposited.

Inventory Report Volatility

Market data releases can lead to sharp, immediate price repricing as the market digests new supply and demand expectations.

OPEC+ And Policy Risk

Market shocks can involve surprise policy decisions, quota changes or disputes between OPEC+ members.

Geopolitical And Weekend Gap Risk

Energy markets are sensitive to world events and prices can “gap” significantly when markets reopen after weekend conflicts or shipping disruptions.

Spread And Execution Risk

Spreads can widen dramatically on low liquidity or breaking news. In fast markets, slippage or execution delay can be a factor too.

Overnight Financing Risk

If your position is open when the daily rollover takes place, overnight financing charges may apply. These can add up and affect the cost of holding a position for longer periods.

Benchmark Risk

WTI and Brent rarely move in unison. If you don’t know what you are trading against, it is very easy to misinterpret the market data. Have a good knowledge of how leverage on oil CFDs works.

Crude Oil CFDs Vs Futures And Physical Oil

CFDs generally do not have the exchange-standardised expiries or physical delivery requirements associated with physical oil and traditional futures, providing a way to gain exposure to price movements through a trading platform.

Oil CFD vs Physical Oil

With a CFD you’re just exposed to the price through the contract. Physical oil is the real thing and needs complex storage, transport and logistics.

Oil CFD Vs Crude Oil Futures

Futures are a standardized contract traded on an exchange and have a maturity date. CFDs are platform-specific and while they usually provide more flexible sizing, they have different counterparty and financing risks.

Common Mistakes Beginners Make With Oil CFDs

New traders struggle because they underestimate the harsh impact of leverage, neglect scheduled inventory data and confuse WTI with Brent.

Mistakes Checklist

  • Assuming oil CFDs mean owning actual barrels of oil.
  • Confusing WTI and Brent dynamics.
  • Not checking which benchmark the platform is tracking.
  • Ignoring the timing of scheduled US inventory reports
  • Trade on OPEC+ headlines without understanding how volatile the reaction can be.
  • Using leverage with no idea about the risks of margin.
  • Not checking spread and overnight fees.
  • Forgetting about the weekend gap risk.
  • Assuming live results are assured on the basis of demo performance.
  • Using random take-profit and stop-loss levels.
  • Treating oil like gold or silver, without understanding the drivers of energy.

FAQs

A crude oil CFD (contract for difference) is a derivative contract that tracks the price of oil but does not give you ownership of physical oil. The result is calculated on the difference between the opening and closing prices, before spread and any financing charges. CFDs are leveraged products and can result in losses that exceed your initial investment .

Indian residents can get information on CFD products and market mechanics online. But , the availability of market access, specific product terms and regulatory availability is dependent on the provider and the individual’s circumstances.

WTI is the US benchmark for crude oil and is closely tied to supply and storage in the country. Brent is the international benchmark and reflects global supply and demand, shipping routes and geopolitical conditions more directly.

Prices are sensitive to OPEC+ production quotas, US inventories, global macroeconomic demand, geopolitical tension, refinery maintenance, seasonal consumption and the value of the US dollar.

The chart shows the past price action in dollars per barrel. It allows new traders to see the direction of the trend, its strength, the position of support and resistance levels and how the market reacts to major news. Past price action does not indicate future movement.

Conclusion

Retail traders can take positions on global energy markets through crude oil CFDs, subject to the risks associated with leverage, volatility and benchmarks. The essential point is that these instruments give price exposure without ownership.

Start by understanding the difference between WTI and Brent and the fundamental forces moving the market such as OPEC+ policies, US inventories, geopolitics and seasonal demand.

Product specifications may vary from provider to provider and leverage does increase potential losses. Traders should read the contract specifications before trading the product. There is never a guarantee in any trade and proper risk management is still key.

Risk warning

CFDs are complex financial instruments and carry a high risk of losing money rapidly due to leverage. You should ensure you fully understand the risks involved and carefully consider whether you can afford to take the high risk of losing your money before trading.

A crude oil CFD is an agreement between you and the provider. It gives you no ownership of, or entitlement to, physical oil, and you are exposed to the provider’s ability to meet its obligations. Margin is collateral against your position and can be lost in full. If your margin level falls below the required level, positions may be closed automatically, and automatic closure does not guarantee a particular exit level. Standard stop-loss orders are not guaranteed and may be executed at a worse level than the one selected, particularly where the price gaps. Financing charges normally apply to positions held overnight.
