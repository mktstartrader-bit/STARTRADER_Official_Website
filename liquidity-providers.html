FAQ

Liquidity and execution questions

What is a tier-1 liquidity provider?
A tier-1 provider is a major bank or institutional market maker that quotes prices directly in the interbank market. Aggregating several of them means orders are filled against a deeper pool of prices than a single counterparty could offer.
Why does the number of providers matter?
More providers competing to fill an order generally means a tighter spread and a better chance of filling at the price you expected, particularly around volatile periods when a single source may widen or step away.
Does deep liquidity guarantee my fill price?
No. Deep liquidity improves the odds of a good fill but no broker can guarantee a price. In fast markets, gapping and slippage can still occur, and stop orders can be filled worse than the level you set.
Are these providers my counterparty?
No. You contract with the STARTRADER entity your account is registered with, as named in your client agreement. The providers supply pricing and liquidity to us, not to you directly.
How does this affect what I pay?
Aggregated tier-1 pricing is what allows spreads from 0.0 pips on ECN accounts, with the cost taken as commission instead. The trading account page sets out the spread and commission structure for each account type.
Where can I see execution details?
Order execution type and account conditions are published on the trading account page, and the terms that apply are set out in the Client Agreement on the legal documents page.
