Gold fails to hold above $4,100 as the Dollar index approaches its yearly high

Bullion gave back the morning’s gains as the greenback firmed into the European close. Here are the levels that matter into the FOMC decision — and what a break of the yearly high would mean for metals.

STARTRADER Research 28.07.2026 · 5 min read
Link copied

Gold spent the Asian session grinding higher and the European session giving it all back. The metal traded as high as $4,118 before the Dollar index found a bid, and by the London close bullion was changing hands under $4,100 again — the third time this month that buyers have failed to hold the handle.

What happened

The move had little to do with gold itself. The Dollar index pushed to within a quarter of a percent of its yearly high after a firmer-than-expected consumer confidence print, and metals priced in dollars did what they usually do when the denominator strengthens. Yields at the front end of the US curve added three basis points, which removed the other prop under the move.

Volume tells the same story. The push above $4,100 came on thinning participation into the European open, and the reversal came on roughly twice the volume — the signature of a level being defended rather than reclaimed.

The levels that matter

Three prices frame the next few sessions. Traders watching for continuation should treat the first as the line in the sand:

  • $4,118Today’s high and the third rejection this month. A close above it puts the range high back in play.
  • $4,062The 20-day moving average, which has caught every dip since the start of the quarter.
  • $4,015The base of the July range. Losing it would turn the recent consolidation into a distribution pattern.

The pattern here is not weakness in gold — it is strength in the Dollar. Until the index turns, rallies in the metal keep meeting the same ceiling.

STARTRADER Research

Into the FOMC

Wednesday’s Federal Reserve decision is the obvious catalyst. The rate itself is not in question — the market has been priced for no change for weeks — so the move will come from the statement language and the press conference that follows half an hour later.

Two things matter for metals. First, any softening in the description of the labour market would pull front-end yields lower and take pressure off gold immediately. Second, and less discussed, is the balance-sheet language: a slower run-off would be read as incrementally dovish and would likely cap the Dollar index short of its yearly high.

Our economic calendar has the decision at 18:00 GMT with the press conference at 18:30. Expect spreads to widen in the seconds around both.

What would change the picture

A daily close above $4,118 with the Dollar index rejecting its yearly high would be the cleanest bullish signal available, because it would mean the metal is advancing without help from the currency. Failing that, the more likely path is continued range trade between the 20-day average and today’s high until the Fed speaks.

On the downside, a break of $4,015 would matter more than the number suggests. It would complete a lower high and a lower low on the daily chart for the first time since April, and it would put the quarter’s uptrend in question rather than merely pausing it.

Trading it responsibly

Ranges compress before events like this one, and compression resolves quickly. Position sizes that feel comfortable in a quiet session become uncomfortable in the first minute after a policy statement, so size for the volatility you expect rather than the volatility you can currently see. Stops placed just beyond an obvious level are the ones most likely to be swept in the initial reaction.
